Arms at your sides. Make big, full circles with your shoulders — up, back, down, forward. 10 reps backward (most important direction), then 10 forward. Feel the full range.

Cat-Cow

10
slow reps
✓
▸ How to do it
On hands and knees. Inhale — drop belly, lift head and tailbone (cow). Exhale — round spine up, tuck chin and pelvis (cat). Move with your breath. Spend 2 seconds at each end. This mobilizes every segment of your spine.

✓
▸ How to do it
Stand feet hip-width apart, hands on hips. Make large circles with your pelvis — like a slow hula hoop. 10 circles each direction. You're warming up tight hip flexors that drive your anterior pelvic tilt.

10

✓
▸ How to do it
Sit cross-legged or on a chair. Cross arms over chest. Rotate your torso to look over your right shoulder, hold 1 sec, return, then left. Keep hips still — this is all mid-back rotation. Critical for rounded shoulders.

Deep Stretching

Stretch

01
Doorway Chest Stretch
Pec major & minor · anterior shoulder
3×60
sec holds
✓
▸ How to do it
Stand in a doorway. Place forearms on the door frame at 90° (like a goalpost). Step one foot through and lean gently forward until you feel the stretch across your chest. Don't force it — hold 60 sec. This is your most important stretch — tight pecs are the primary driver of your rounded shoulders.
REST — 30 sec between sets
02
Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch
Iliopsoas · rectus femoris
3×60
sec each side
✓
▸ How to do it
Kneel on one knee (right knee down, left foot forward). Tuck your pelvis under — squeeze your right glute and posteriorly tilt your pelvis. Then shift your weight forward gently. You should feel a deep stretch at the front of your right hip. Do NOT arch your back. This directly addresses your anterior pelvic tilt.
REST — 30 sec between sets
03
Chin Tuck + Neck Flexor Stretch
Suboccipitals · SCM · upper traps
3×45
sec holds
✓
▸ How to do it
Stand or sit tall. Tuck your chin back (creating a double chin — yes really). Then gently add a slight downward nod. You'll feel tension at the base of your skull and the back of your neck. Hold. This lengthens the short muscles that pull your head forward.
REST — 30 sec between sets
04
Upper Trap Stretch
Upper trapezius · levator scapulae
3×45
sec each side
✓
▸ How to do it
Sit or stand. Drop your right ear toward your right shoulder. Place your right hand lightly on top of your head — don't pull, just let gravity help. You'll feel a stretch up the left side of your neck. Hold. Switch sides. These muscles get chronically tight with forward head posture.
REST — 30 sec between sets
05
Thread the Needle
Thoracic spine · rhomboids · posterior shoulder
3×45
sec each side
✓
▸ How to do it
On hands and knees. Slide your right arm along the floor under your left arm, letting your right shoulder and temple drop to the floor. Left arm stays planted. Feel the rotation and stretch through your mid-back and behind your right shoulder. Great for thoracic mobility.
REST — 30 sec between sets
06
Quadratus Lumborum Stretch
QL · lateral lower back
2×60
sec each side
✓
▸ How to do it
Stand feet wide. Reach your right arm up and over to the left, side-bending your torso. Left hand on hip for support. Keep hips square and facing forward — don't rotate. You should feel this on the right side of your lower back/hip. The QL gets tight with anterior pelvic tilt.

Activation

Wake up inhibited, underactive muscles

~10 min
Activate
▾
01
Chin Tucks (wall)
Deep neck flexors · longus colli
3×15
3 sec holds
✓
▸ How to do it
Stand with your back against a wall, heels 2 inches from the wall. Press your entire back flat. Now retract your chin (making a double chin) and try to touch the back of your head to the wall. Hold 3 seconds. Release. This activates the deep neck flexors that are completely shut off in forward head posture.
REST — 45 sec between sets
02
Scapular Wall Slides
Lower traps · serratus anterior
3×12
slow reps
✓
▸ How to do it
Stand against a wall. Press your lower back, upper back, and head into the wall. Place arms in a "W" position — elbows bent, backs of hands touching the wall. Slowly slide arms up into a "Y" (overhead), keeping ALL contact points on the wall. Lower back down to W. This is extremely hard if you have rounded shoulders — that's the point.
REST — 60 sec between sets
03
Dead Bug
Transverse abdominis · deep core
3×10
each side
✓
▸ How to do it
Lie on your back, arms pointing to the ceiling, knees bent at 90° in the air (like a dead bug). PRESS your lower back completely flat into the floor — this is essential. Slowly extend your right arm overhead and left leg down simultaneously, hovering just above the floor. Return. Switch sides. If your back arches at all, reduce range. This trains the deep core that's disengaged with your pelvic tilt.
REST — 60 sec between sets
04
Glute Bridge
Glutes · hamstrings · posterior chain
3×15
3 sec squeeze
✓
▸ How to do it
Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart. Press through your heels and squeeze your glutes hard as you drive your hips up. At the top, tuck your pelvis under (posterior tilt) and hold for 3 seconds — really squeeze. Your glutes are inhibited by tight hip flexors, and this directly counters anterior pelvic tilt.
REST — 60 sec between sets
04

Strengthening

Build postural muscle endurance

~18 min
Strengthen
▾
01
Band Pull-Aparts
Rear delts · rhomboids · mid traps
4×20
1 sec squeeze
✓
▸ How to do it
Hold a resistance band (or towel) at shoulder height with straight arms, hands slightly wider than shoulders. Pull the band apart, spreading it across your chest, squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end. Hold 1 second. Return slowly. No band? Use a rolled-up bedsheet. This is the single best exercise for rounded shoulders.
REST — 60 sec between sets
02
YTW raises (prone)
Lower traps · mid traps · rear delts
3×10
each letter
✓
▸ How to do it
Lie face down on your bed or floor, arms overhead. Y — raise arms to a Y shape (overhead diagonal). T — arms straight out to the sides. W — elbows bent at 90°, pull back like a flex. Hold each position 2 seconds at the top. These muscles are chronically weak in round-shoulder posture and almost never trained.
REST — 60 sec between sets
03
Bird-Dog
Erector spinae · glutes · core stability
3×12
each side · 3 sec hold
✓
▸ How to do it
On hands and knees, spine neutral (not arched, not rounded). Extend your right arm forward and left leg back simultaneously. Hold for 3 seconds — fight to keep your hips perfectly level. Don't rotate. Return and switch. This builds the paraspinal muscles that support upright posture.
REST — 60 sec between sets
04
Single-Leg Glute Bridge
Glutes · hip stabilizers
3×12
each leg · 2 sec hold
✓
▸ How to do it
Same as the glute bridge but extend one leg straight. Drive through the planted heel. Your hips will want to drop to the side — resist that. Hold 2 seconds at the top with a hard glute squeeze. Harder than it sounds. Builds unilateral glute strength to correct pelvic imbalances.
REST — 60 sec between sets
05
Hollow Body Hold
Anterior core · hip flexors (lengthened)
3×30
sec
✓
▸ How to do it
Lie on your back. Posteriorly tilt your pelvis — flatten your lower back into the floor. Legs extended to 45°, arms overhead. Hold this hollow shape. If your back peels off the floor, raise your legs higher. This trains anterior core in a way that directly counteracts lower back arching from your pelvic tilt.
REST — 60 sec between sets
06
Prone Cobra
Erector spinae · lower traps · glutes
3×15
3 sec holds
✓
▸ How to do it
Lie face down, arms at your sides, palms facing the floor. Squeeze your glutes, then lift your chest off the floor while rotating your arms so palms face outward (thumbs up). Squeeze shoulder blades together and DOWN. Hold 3 seconds. Lower. This is a full postural chain exercise — back extensors, lower traps, glutes all fire together.

Integration

Repattern how you hold your body

~6 min
Integrate
▾
01
Wall Posture Hold
Full postural alignment
3×60
sec
✓
▸ How to do it
Stand with heels, calves, glutes, mid-back, and back of head all touching the wall. Feet 2 inches from wall. Chin tucked. Arms at sides, palms facing forward. Breathe normally and hold. This teaches your nervous system what neutral posture feels like. It will feel weird — that weirdness means it's working. After the hold, step away from the wall and try to maintain that same feeling while walking around.
REST — 45 sec between sets
02
Posture Walking
Gait integration · proprioception
3
min walk
✓
▸ How to do it
Walk around your room with deliberate posture: chin tucked, ears over shoulders, chest open, glutes slightly engaged, belly button pulled in gently. Take slow, deliberate steps. This isn't exercise — it's neurological reprogramming. You're creating a new default. The goal is to feel what correct posture feels like while moving.

▸ How to do it

✓
▸ How to do it
Lie on your back. Bring right knee to chest, then gently guide it across your body to the left. Arms out in a T, both shoulders stay on the floor. Look right. Feel the rotation through your spine. Breathe and let gravity do the work.
REST — 30 sec
03
Diaphragmatic Breathing
Nervous system · postural reset
10
deep breaths
✓
▸ How to do it
Lie on your back. Place one hand on your chest, one on your belly. Breathe in for 4 counts — only the hand on your belly should rise. Breathe out slowly for 6 counts. Correct breathing is actually a posture issue — chest breathing keeps upper traps tense all day. Belly breathing is the finish line.
